## The Game

![The Adventures of Batman & Robin logo](/games/adventures-of-batman-and-robin/logo.png)

*The Adventures of Batman & Robin* is based on the 1992 series *Batman: The Animated Series*, which was retitled in its second season to *The Adventures of Batman & Robin*. A game of the same name was also released on Genesis/Mega Drive, Game Gear, Sega CD, and Game Boy, but none of them were ports of the other, each game was a completely different thing with different developers and publishers, so no comparisons to be made here!

Developed and published by the one and only **Konami**, their first entry on our list, and what really needs to be said about **Konami**? One of the biggest names in the arcade world in the early 80s, before breaking into consoles in a huge way with series like *Castlevania*, *Metal Gear*/*Metal Gear Solid*, *Contra*, you could keep going and going, and they were already very well established in 1994, *Turtles in Time* released 3 years prior and is considered one of the best beat-em-ups on the console.

### Gameplay

*The Adventures of Batman & Robin* does more than just your standard beat-em-up, when you get a typical beat-em-up level it is fairly standard, in fact mechanically it's very basic. Walk left to right, one button to attack which is either a punch when you're stood up, a kick when you're crouched, or a flying kick when you're jumping, you can also walk into enemies and grab them to then throw them. No combos, just one attack button and some of Batman's gadgets. The extra weapons like the gas grenades and the sonic batarang I never really used, it's a bit fiddly pressing a button to cycle through gadgets and then use it to attack all while people are coming at you. But what this game does do well is the variety, you start out with a typical level, then you end up fighting The Joker on a rollercoaster as it's whizzing along the tracks. Then you're in a museum with 3 levels, and you have to find members of staff that The Penguin has tied up, this level is all about exploration, going up and down the elevator, checking in with Robin by using a phone on the wall so he can tell you how many are left and where about in the building they are. Then there's a Batmobile level, and Riddler's maze that has you taking lefts and rights trying to find Commissioner Gordon all while The Riddler is asking you riddles, The Scarecrow's level has innocent people taken over by his gas attacking you, Batman can't kick the shit out of them so he has a spray to knock them unconscious. There's so much variety and the game never gets boring despite the basic combat.

One thing I did find was the bosses, despite looking cool and having some interesting arenas, had far too much health, once the pattern had been learned you just find yourself jumping around, not getting hit, and punching for ages before the boss is done for. The bosses are all cool though, you fight through Batman's typical rogues gallery, Joker, Penguin, Two Face, Poison Ivy, Riddler, Catwoman, then what I really liked was the final level is essentially a boss rush, but they throw in some of the villains you haven't fought before, Clayface and Man Bat. You can tell a lot of love and thought has gone into this.

There are some things I didn't like though, I mentioned before about the gadgets, some you use as part of the level and it's quite cool, a torch in the museum when the power goes out so you can see land mines, X-Ray goggles in Riddler's maze so you can see through walls, and a gas mask in Scarecrow's stage to avoid his gas. The weapons though were mostly ignored. One of the other gadgets you have is a grappling hook, and the idea is great, grapple to a surface, swing forward and jump off *Bionic Commando* style, problem is it's really frustrating to use and there are a couple of levels that require it to be used pretty much perfectly. One of the other issues is the wall jump, now I know this game is 32 years old, and wall jumping hadn't been as popular in games as it is now, but it was so awkward to get right, and one level makes you do it to progress, except there's vents on the walls that damage you, so you have to time your jumps to avoid them, except you have no control over the jump arc once the jump happens, so you have to get hit, drop down and try again, hoping you start in the right spot to avoid them. If you haven't seen it, check out [Rob's Wheel of Shame video](https://youtu.be/q8tbjeWi5yU?si=aZuGS0ONMC39XOVF) on Speedrunners, that reaction to wall jumping is about the same as the reaction I was having when I played this.

### Graphics

Graphics are phenomenal, it's like they were lifted straight from the animated series, each level starts at the bat computer talking to Alfred or Gordon, or watching a news report, and all the characters look exactly like they do in the show, it has really nice fluid animations, Batman's cape flowing behind you when you run, or billowing above you when you fall, there's some really nice parallax scrolling in some levels, and a really gorgeous scene with you chasing Catwoman across the roof tops while search lights shine on you from below. There's colour, there's darkness, a great mix of everything, the only thing I wish there was more of was enemy variety. What you have looks great, but when you fight your 100th Tommy Gun wielding gangster it starts to look a little bit less impressive. The bosses are all designed really well, you fight on a rollercoaster, the wings of a plane, a chessboard that breaks away under your feet as you run. These games based on animated series really do lend themselves well to this era of gaming. I did get a bit of slow down in places, so either the SNES was being pushed to its limits, or my emulator was crapping out.

### Sound and Music

Really nice recreation of the theme song for the show, and a decent soundtrack to go along with it. The tracks are all quite short so while they are good, you can get repetition if you linger on a level too long. The levels are fairly short so it doesn't happen often. It does make you feel like you're playing an episode of the show.

Sound is about what you'd expect for a beat-em-up, grunts and groans as you kick seven shades of shit out of a villain's goons. Nothing majorly stand out, but nothing offensive that will have you turning the volume down. Weirdly the boss fight with The Scarecrow seemed to have the exact same sound when you hit him versus when you got hit, so it was hard to tell if I was landing a blow or getting hit myself. Not sure if that was an intentional thing or a mistake, but it was odd.

## The People

A lot of people worked on this game! 7 people on sound effects and music alone. **Jun Funahashi** had previously worked on *TMNT* for the NES, *Castlevania III*, and *Batman Returns* (had some experience with Batman music already!) among other. After this he went on to work on a lot of modern Frogger games of all things, and his last game was *Stranglehold* in 2007. **Kazuhiko Uehara** was obviously a mainstay for **Konami**, working on *Metal Gear 2*, *Turtles in Time*, *Parodius*, and he had a great credit on *Metal Gear Solid 2: Substance* as part of the "Skateboarding unit"

6 people worked on the graphics, **Yoichi Yoshimoto** was credited on the most other games, he'd mainly worked on smaller NES titles or Japanese exclusives, but after this went on to work on *Brave Fencer Musashi* for PS1, various *Kingdom Hearts* games, *Final Fantasy IV: The Complete Collection* on PSP, and *Mario Sports Mix* on the Wii.

The game also had 4 programmers, obviously not a case of too many cooks spoiling the broth because the game came out great. **Makoto Sato** still works in the industry today programming a companion app for *Final Fantasy XIV*.

All these Japanese people working hard on such a good game, and it never even released in Japan.
