## The Game

![Aerobiz logo](/games/aerobiz/logo.png)

*Aerobiz* was developed and published by **Koei** who are well known for their strategy/tactics games, from the 80s through the 00s with series like *Romance of the Three Kingdoms*, *Nobunaga's Ambition*, *Pacific Theatre of Operations*, and many many more. Most games of theirs were very highly received, and to anyone into these sorts of games they will be very well known, despite some of their games going more under the radar, such as this one. It was also released for the Mega Drive/Genesis, and various Japanese home computers.

### Gameplay

This is a pretty in depth strategy game, you take control of an airline and have to make various business decisions to ensure you are the number one airline out of the 4 people playing, and yes, this can have a full 4 player scenario running which I think would've been great fun at the time. If you don't have other players the seats are filled by CPU players and the actual gameplay experience is the same. There are 2 scenarios to choose from, start in 1963 - 1995 or 1983-2015. I went with 1983 so I could get myself a nice fleet of Concordes. Yes, this game is fully licensed (not the sort of licensed game I thought I'd be dealing with on the SNES, where is the early 90s box office flop movie tie in?!) Boeing, Concorde, Airbus all licensed for this game which is a nice little feature.

You start by choosing your base of operations, which is basically your way of choosing difficulty. Pick a big city, New York, London, Tokyo etc and you'll have an easier time, but choosing smaller places, or somewhere like Moscow will make your game a lot harder. You then buy your planes, and negotiate with other airports to open routes, set your ticket prices and your game starts there.

It's like *Sim City* or *Theme Park*, but it feels more in depth despite not actually really being all that much more in depth. You have board meetings, financial reports and all that great spreadsheet stuff you get from strategy games like this, and there are real world issues that crop up. War in Iran raises oil prices, the Olympic Games will drastically increase travel to the host country so you need to consider opening more routes in advance. You can dabble in marketing and open hotels and new bases of operations in other countries to link everything together with the ultimate goal being to earn the most money, and have the most customers while trying to link all 22 cities together.

You can lose the game by going bankrupt or hitting the 32 year time limit. It's a great little niche title, and the fact it's just so different than everything else I've played recently was nice. A well deserved break from middle of the road platformers.

### Graphics

The game looks nice for what it is, but there isn't much to talk about when it comes to the graphics. The Japanese home computer versions obviously looked the best, but the SNES does a nice job of showing you a nice world map, decent character sprites for the various people you talk to and nice bold clear icons for all the sections you navigate through.

### Sound & Music

There isn't much to talk about in the way of sound, but the music in the game is pretty decent. 35 minute soundtrack, so maybe not a huge amount of variety, but what's there is good. You get so lost in the gameplay that you don't really concentrate much on the music though it has to be said, but a listen to the soundtrack without playing the game does showcase some really nice upbeat tracks that are very nicely put together.

## The People

Now this is a weird one, the only person I can find credited on this is the game's producer **Kou Shibusawa**. He was one of the co-founders of **Koei**, and has worked on over 100 games as a producer but also was behind the story of 2017's souls like *Nioh*.

The opening credits of the game just lists the various licenses they have for the planes in the game, and the ending is just a series of digitised images of planes while music plays. There just doesn't seem to be any credits anywhere for it. If you know of any, or if **Mr Shibusawa** is reading this, if you'd like to drop me a list of the people that worked on this obscure SNES version of a PC strategy game from 34 years ago that would be great!
