## The Game

![Aero the Acro-Bat 2 logo](/games/aero-the-acro-bat-2/logo.png)

*Aero the Acro-Bat 2* was developed and published by the same team that made *Aero the Acro-Bat*, so there isn't much more to say about them here, and I think we'll have the same situation when it comes to the people that worked on the game. **Iguana** seemed to be loyal to their staff and keep them working on their games.

### Gameplay

The core gameplay remains the same, but I'm very happy to say the issues I had with the first game have mostly been tightened up and fixed with this game, there's a fairly coherent story told by cut scenes between levels, and while it does get a bit strange, it's nice to have a reason for what you're doing. It takes place straight after the first game and has Aero rescuing a female bat that is very heavily implied to have been held as a sex slave, who turns out to be a double agent, finding his band (apparently he's in a band as well as an acrobatic circus troupe), rescuing his circus friends, and fighting the game's main antagonist at the end to round things off.

The weird slippery feeling has been fixed, and while the game does still feel a touch too fast at times, especially later on when enemies can literally just pop up out of the ground, it definitely feels a lot better. The attack is the same angled corkscrew attack, but it feels more responsive this time thankfully, and the worlds aren't anywhere near as long winded as the first game.

That said I do feel like the game outstayed its welcome slightly, the final world felt like one world too many, but this is someone from 2026 talking about a game released over 30 years ago, maybe if this was the only game I was going to get for 6 months I'd want it to be as long as possible, but the last worlds had a fairly big difficulty spike, and one that felt almost unfair, multiple enemies appearing out of the ground on the same screen, enemies that spew fire at you that you can barely jump high enough to get over, platforms that spew fire, one hit kill swinging spiky rocks, it just all got really hard very suddenly, when everything before it was a nice challenge, but an enjoyable one.

Gone are the *Battletoads* like auto scrollers, spikes aren't an instant kill anymore, pretty much everything I didn't like about the first game was fixed in this game so the game deserves a higher score, it's just a shame that there's still something about it that doesn't quite gel with me. When I play a *Mario* game, every time I finish a level I'm excited to see what the next level brings, I want to move on to the next world and see what the theme is, I'll replay levels for collectibles, I had none of that feel with this game, I can't even put my finger exactly on why, that desire just isn't there for it.

### Graphics

Once again however, the game is absolutely fantastic to look at, even more so than the first game. Really nice parallax scrolling effects, the characters, the enemies, the levels, they all look fantastic. The art used for the "cut scenes" is really nice too, although I do think Aero in "cut scene" form does look really bizarre, but I think that's maybe just a personal preference.

Varied enemy sprites throughout the whole game this time, levels vibrant and colourful with music themes, caves, a moving train, they all look fantastic. My favourite world was the music themed world, it reminded me of the music themed world in *Rayman*, running along sheet music, jumping on CD platforms, it's one of the best looking platformers on the system for sure.

### Sound & Music

Once again when it comes to the sound there isn't too much to say, typical platformer noises, pretty fun but standard stuff. Thankfully they changed the noise made when you collect an extra life for it, and it now sounds like a good thing when you get one.

The soundtrack is miles better for this game, over an hour long with 18 tracks definitely gives you a much better average length of the tracks, and while some do have a loop, you don't really notice while you're playing because the tune is put together so well. I'm not sure, but I felt like some of the songs were based on real life songs, but I could never quite put my finger on which ones. Head and shoulders above the "I hope you like circus music" theme of the first game, and a soundtrack I could happily have on in the background when not playing the game, which is the scale I use on whether I think the soundtrack is good or not. So a strong win here.
