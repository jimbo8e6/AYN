## The Game

![A.S.P Air Strike Patrol logo](/games/a-s-p/logo.png)

*A.S.P - Air Strike Patrol* is a top down action flight simulator which does try a few things to separate it from the shoot-em-ups of the era. Also known as Desert Fighter in European regions so it should have been a lot later down the line seeing as I'm in the UK, but I used an American-centric list to get my list of games before I realised this, so now I have to commit to the American names for games in this review series because I'm nothing if not consistent.

It was developed by **Opus Studios**, who were a Japanese developer established in 1990, and only just closed down a couple of years ago in 2024 — they had a long career but no real standout titles. One of the games they developed was *Half Minute Hero*, which is a fantastic game that I urge people to try out. It was published by **SETA**, who were also involved in the publishing of a lot of games with no real standouts. The USA branch SETA USA was also credited in the publishing of the game, and they did publish quite a few SNES games, but again nothing really outstanding.

### Gameplay

Set in a fictional version of the Gulf War against a country called Zaraq — a little bit on the nose, but it's fine! It's an isometric shooter, and you pilot one of two different aircraft: an A-10 or an F-15, each having slightly different play styles. As you can imagine the A-10 is slower and more focused on bombing runs, while the F-15 is faster and well suited for air to air combat.

Jumping in I expected a typical top down shoot-em-up — you get dropped in to an area, blow it all to pieces, come home victorious. So I'm flying my F-15 and I see some tanks, I fire off some air to ground missiles, wipe them out, then I see some buildings and I wonder how good the damage modelling is. Can all the buildings be destroyed? The answer is yes! I didn't really know what I was doing, I'd jumped in without reading anything, so I flew off the side of the map which ended the mission and sent me back to HQ. When this happens you get a news broadcast screen pop up to tell you how you did. This particular time they seemed quite keen on discussing huge amounts of civilian casualties and friendly fire…

So it turns out the battlefield has your own troops on it, it has civilian towns, and you are tasked with destroying very specific things in a certain amount of time. Who knew?

What I'd missed was the pre-mission intel screen that tells you all about what you need to do, shows you the sprites of your allies, your enemies, your targets and so on. Armed with this knowledge I went back in and started to have a great time.

The feel of the planes is really nice — you have speed controls and an actual turning circle in the air, so when you're in a dogfight with an enemy fighter you have to think about slowing down and speeding up to try and get behind them. When you're on a bombing run in the A-10 you need to consider the time remaining on your mission, it being a much slower and more methodical gameplay style. I did find most of the missions were geared toward bombing runs, so the A-10 was used mostly, although the F-15 was really good for a quick recon. You get a certain amount of in-game hours to complete your mission, and you can do multiple flyovers in that time — so I'd go out in the F-15, take down as many enemy MiGs as possible while searching the map for the objectives, then once I had the lay of the land I'd come back out in the A-10 and start the bombing runs.

Not only do you have to consider the time, but your fuel and ammo are limited in each run, so you really have to make every shot count if you want to complete the mission on schedule. I really liked the way this all felt — it almost gave the game a sense of realism and a bit of planning. Failed missions don't end the game, but they can make later missions harder, make people start to dislike you, and can even affect the ending.

Everything in the game just feels satisfying, especially the bombing runs — bombing a huge refinery and seeing all the destruction in your wake never got old. So while the gameplay loop is repetitive, it's so much fun it didn't really bother me. The targets were visually distinct enough, and the story was intriguing enough to keep you pushing on.
