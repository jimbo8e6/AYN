## The Game

Licensed platformers are the background radiation of the SNES library, and most
of them are exactly what you expect. *The Addams Family* is not. Ocean built it
as a single connected mansion — a hub you wander, with wings and floors that
unlock as you find your way in — rather than a menu of levels. You play Gomez,
looking for the rest of the family, and the game largely trusts you to work out
where to go.

That trust is also the problem. There is no map. There are no directions worth
the name. The mansion loops back on itself in ways that are genuinely clever
once you have it in your head, and genuinely maddening for the several hours
before that. Combine it with a finite stock of lives and you get a game that
punishes exploration, which is an odd thing for an exploration game to do.

What holds up is the craft. The animation has real character, the mansion has a
consistent visual logic to it, and the controls are precise enough that deaths
feel earned rather than arbitrary. Ocean clearly cared.

## The People

**Ocean Software**, out of Manchester, were one of the biggest names in European
games in this period, and film licences were a large part of how they got there.
Their reputation was mixed for exactly the reason all licence houses' were —
the deadline is the release date of the film, not the date the game is ready.

This one lands on the better end of their output, which is worth saying out
loud given how much Ocean-bashing became reflexive later.
