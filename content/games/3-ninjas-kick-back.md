## The Game

3 Ninjas Kick Back is a licensed action platformer, which you'll discover going
forwards was the 90s console's bread and butter! If it was even remotely
popular, it had a game, and a lot of the time that game was an action
platformer. This game is the perfect example of just what sort of things they
turned into video games at the time. The film this was based on released the
same year and had a worldwide gross box office of $11,798,854, which adjusted
for inflation is around $25 million. For reference, that would be like if
*28 Years Later: The Bone Temple* or *Primate* had video game counterparts in
2026. Licensed games were everywhere!

### Gameplay

As for the game itself, you choose one of the three titular ninjas: Colt, Rocky
or Tum Tum. Each one has a different weapon (children running round with swords
and sai in the 90s was all okay, I guess!) but they all play identically, which
removes any replayability from the game completely, and the game then loosely
follows along with the plot of the movie.

It's set across 5 worlds broken up into different levels, and there is a nice
bit of variety in the mechanics of the levels. You have your straightforward
point A to point B, left to right, platform levels, you have more open platform
levels where you have to think about where you're going and how to get there,
and levels where you're being chased by a giant rolling rock and you have to
keep on the move. The variety in these styles does break the game up fairly
well, but ultimately it does still get quite samey as time goes on. The game is
simple in its design, but overly challenging in its execution, which was pretty
common for the time to discourage someone renting the game and finishing it in
an evening. With save states I was able to finish the game in just over an hour,
which, for a full-priced game, is nowhere near good enough! So artificially
inflate the difficulty, keep the players trying and increase the play time of
the game.

The thing I found strange about this game was that one level was really detailed
and full of quite cool interactive elements — throwing enemies into electrical
fixtures, dropping things from the background onto their heads, pillows
exploding when you slashed them with your sword — but every other level was just
some sort of brown, outdoor borefest, and it just seemed like all their eggs
were in one basket and by world 4 someone has just thrown that basket out the
window.

I'm writing as though I really dislike the game though, and that's not entirely
true. It's competent enough, and it's not broken. I went in with zero
expectations considering the movie tie-in nature of the game and ultimately it
was better than I thought, but not one I'd play again. Worth a look if you enjoy
simple action platformers.

### Graphics

Graphically the game is mostly pretty nice. The sprites are big and bold, the
animations are nice and smooth, and the levels are fairly nicely detailed to
look at despite most of them not having that interaction that one level had. The
boss sprites are really nice, especially the boss of the Hospital level, a giant
nurse brandishing a syringe at a literal child. There are certainly much better
graphics on the SNES, but for a game released in the middle of the console's
life cycle it could certainly have done a lot worse!

### Sound and music

The sound effects are pretty basic fare for a game like this, with a small bit
of digitised speech when you select your character probably being the technical
highlight. The music, however, really had some promise. Each stage had some
nice, catchy, and well put together music that was so close to getting a seal of
approval from me and pushing this game into recommendation territory.
Unfortunately, the problem with it is that it loops too soon, so you end up with
a decently put together piece of music that ends up driving you crazy before the
level is over! This is a real shame because it was so close, and it had every
right to have an amazing soundtrack considering the man behind it...

## The People

**Sony Imagesoft** is the interesting name here. This was Sony's games
publishing arm before the PlayStation existed, and it spent the early nineties
putting out licensed titles on Nintendo and Sega hardware. By 1994 Sony had
already fallen out with Nintendo over the abandoned SNES CD-ROM add-on and was
months from launching a console aimed squarely at them — and was still, at the
same time, shipping cartridges for the SNES. There is a whole write-up in that
overlap alone.

**Malibu Interactive** handled development. Less well documented, and worth
digging into.
